Deep Learning for Actuarial Modeling

A credit risk companion

Fourteen lectures read the Milan 2026 summer school through one running example: a consumer probability of default problem on the Bondora peer-to-peer loan book. Eight follow the course lecture for lecture, and five answer questions the course does not ask, on survival analysis, regulatory capital and causation. Every figure and every number on these pages is computed by the lecture that displays it.

The course sequence

Numbered to match the summer school, so lecture 7 here answers lecture 7 there.

1

Introduction and Data Example

Sets up the Bondora loan book as a PD problem, fixes the outcome window and the two exposure conventions, and reads the course's opening lecture through them.

Read·PDF

2

Exponential Dispersion Family and Generalised Linear Models

Works the family with the Bernoulli member in place of the Poisson, then fits a logistic GLM and validates it on a random and an out-of-time split.

Read·PDF

3

Deep Learning, an Overview

Asks what representation learning is for, comparing principal components, an autoencoder and a supervised extractor on a wide credit card portfolio, since Bondora's interpretable columns make no case for it.

Read·PDF

4 & 5

Feed-Forward Neural Networks

Replaces the GLM readout's hand-built features with learned ones, trains the network in PyTorch by stochastic gradient descent with early stopping, and benchmarks it on both splits.

Read·PDF

6

Ensembling and Entity Embedding

Averages twenty networks to take the randomness out of the fit, which buys as much out-of-sample deviance as the move from GLM to network did, then encodes high-cardinality categoricals as learned embeddings.

Read·PDF

7

Balance Property, Auto-Calibration and Lift Charts

Nests three calibration properties, measures the network's 0.69 per cent balance miss against the GLM's exact zero, and asks whether every price cohort finances its own defaults.

Read·PDF

8

ICEnet and Regularisation

Runs ridge, LASSO and group LASSO over the Bondora design matrix, shows that excluding the intercept from the penalty restates the balance property, then imposes monotonicity and smoothness through ICEnet.

Read·PDF

Survival track

Numbered outside the course sequence, because no course lecture answers them. They pick up the discrete-hazard convention lecture 1 defines but never uses.

S1

From a Fixed-Horizon PD to a Discrete Hazard

Derives the survival product that turns monthly hazards into a lifetime PD, and shows a discrete-time hazard model is a logistic regression on a person-period expansion.

Read·PDF

S2

Survival Analysis from Insurance to Credit

Maps the four vocabularies that describe the same mathematics, identifies the central exposed to risk with the person-period denominator, and supplies the Fine-Gray correction S1 defers.

Read·PDF

S3

Neural Survival Heads

Emits one logit per month from a single forward pass, so the whole term structure arrives without materialising 2.7 million person-period rows, then asks lecture 7's calibration questions of it.

Read·PDF

Regulatory track

Also outside the sequence. Both grow out of callouts in lecture 1 that named a regulatory idea and then admitted the fixed-horizon cross-section could not carry it.

R1

Point-in-Time PD and the IFRS 9 Term Structure

Separates the survival conditioning axis from the macroeconomic one, reviews eleven ways to estimate a point-in-time term structure, and fits one against real Eurostat unemployment, inflation and growth.

Read·PDF

R2

Risk Grades, the Long-Run Average and IRB Capital

Runs the five-step production sequence a bank actually runs, from a point-in-time scorecard through grades, a long-run average and a margin of conservatism to the regulatory PD, then on to the risk weight.

Read·PDF

R3

Sample Design, Representativeness and Class Imbalance

Audits the partition every other lecture inherited. Dates the pandemic relief distortion to a two-month notch, shows the fixed-horizon table cannot express a calendar exclusion, finds a 2017 data break, and separates development from out-of-time at an AUC of 0.96.

Read·PDF

Causal track

Lecture 1 made a causal argument correctly and without a single causal word. This track supplies the vocabulary and then spends it.

C1

Interaction, Confounding and What a Coefficient Means

Separates interaction from effect modification, derives an adjustment set from a causal diagram rather than from predictive lift, and prices the exposure to unmeasured confounding at an E-value of 1.34.

Read·PDF

Definitional track

Outside the sequence again. Lecture 1 named default three times without finishing it, and this track owns the response variable the whole series is built on.

D1

The Definition of Default, Cure and Write-off

Separates the three response variables that all answer to the name twelve-month PD, measures Bondora's own declaration lag at a median 79 days past due, and sweeps the delinquency, persistence and probation dials on a monthly panel to show a cure rate running from 61.8 per cent to 37.8.

Read·PDF

Written by Mario Ervedosa alongside Deep Learning for Actuarial Modeling, run at Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore, Milan, from 31 August to 4 September 2026 by Salvatore Scognamiglio, Marco Maggi, Mario V. Wüthrich and Ronald Richman. The course's own storyline and notation are followed throughout, and its lecture notes are on SSRN. The authors' lecture documents are theirs, so they are not published here.

The data is the public Bondora loan book, extract dated 20 July 2021, with three public Eurostat series behind the R track. Lecture 3 and the imbalance section of R3 use a second public set, the anonymised credit card behaviour score development and validation pair. Each lecture renders from a Quarto source, and every displayed figure is computed at render time. No client data is involved anywhere on this site.
